About

I am an Associate Professor of Computer Science and Engineering at the University of Texas at Arlington (UTA). Before joining UTA, I was an Assistant Professor at the State University of New York (SUNY) at Binghamton. I received my Ph.D. from Purdue University, where I conducted research in the FRIENDS Lab under the direction of Professor Dongyan Xu. I am the recipient of an NSF CAREER Award (2023).

My research focuses on making cloud and AI infrastructure faster, safer, and more efficient. I am particularly interested in practical tensions that arise in real systems: how to isolate workloads without giving up performance, how to move data across memory tiers and hosts without disrupting applications, and how to make better use of increasingly heterogeneous hardware. These questions have shaped my work on lightweight secure containers and virtualization, transparent memory tiering across DRAM, CXL, and accelerators, high-throughput networking and storage for cloud workloads, and systems support for large-scale LLM training and inference. Much of this work is motivated by real deployment needs and carried out in collaboration with industry partners. The results have appeared at venues including OSDI, SOSP, EuroSys, and USENIX ATC.
